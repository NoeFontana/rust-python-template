## Context and problem statement

What is the situation, and what forces it a decision? Two or three
paragraphs. State the problem, not the solution. If this decision
triggers a rule from ADR-0001 §"What counts as significant", name
the clause here.

## Decision drivers

- The constraint or goal that most narrows the option space.
- The failure mode this decision is trying to avoid.
- Any hard requirement inherited from an earlier ADR.

## Considered options

If the decision has several orthogonal axes, enumerate them and
number the options per axis (Axis A — 1/2/3, Axis B — 1/2/3). If it
is a single choice, a flat numbered list is fine.

1. **Option one.**
2. **Option two.**
3. **Option three.**

## Decision outcome

Chosen: **option N** (or, for multi-axis decisions, **A2 + B1 + C1**).

Then one subsection per axis explaining the choice and *why the
rejected options were rejected*. The rejections are the part future
readers — human and agent — actually need.

## What this ADR explicitly does *not* decide

Bound the scope. Anything listed here is out of scope and does not
need re-litigating when someone asks "but what about X?".

## Consequences

- **Positive.** What gets better, concretely.
- **Negative.** What this costs. Be honest; an ADR with no negative
  consequences was not a real decision.
- **Neutral.** What changes without being better or worse.
